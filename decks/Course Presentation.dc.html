AI from Zero to Hero

AI shaping FoodTech & SocialTech

AI from Zero to Hero is a hands-on introduction to deep learning for people who want to build with it and judge it. We open with what AI promises and what it has already delivered, from AlphaGo and AlphaFold to NotCo's Giuseppe, which matches plant ingredients to animal-based foods at the molecular level. Then we open the black box: you compute a neuron by hand, implement a two-layer network in NumPy, and rebuild it in TensorFlow. You learn how activation functions shape what a network can represent, how bias and variance tell you what to fix next, and how CNNs, LSTMs and Transformers extend the same ideas to images, text and time series. Every lecture comes with a notebook, so each concept is something you have run, broken and repaired yourself.

What you take away

By the end of the course you can

Place
Locate AI, machine learning and deep learning on the capability spectrum, and explain why deep learning made problems like Go and protein folding solvable.
Compute
Trace how a neuron and a layer turn inputs into outputs, and implement forward and backward propagation for a two-layer network in NumPy.
Build
Define, train and use neural networks in TensorFlow and Keras for binary and multiclass classification, such as handwritten digit recognition.
Choose
Pick activation functions for hidden and output layers, and write softmax and its loss in a numerically stable way.
Diagnose
Split data into training, cross-validation and test sets, tell high bias from high variance, and decide whether to add data, features or regularization.
Apply
Recognize the main architecture families (CNNs, RNNs and LSTMs, Transformers, generative models, detectors) and forecast a real time series with the Temporal Fusion Transformer and Chronos-2.

Syllabus

Five modules, from motivation to modern architectures

Each module maps to a lecture deck in this project and to the lab notebooks that practice it.

Module 1

Introduction & Motivation

Open lecture
  • The AI capability spectrum, from rule-based systems to self-evolving AI
  • How AI, machine learning and deep learning nest inside one another
  • Landmark systems: AlphaGo, AlphaStar, AlphaFold, and the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry
  • Case study: NotCo's Giuseppe, from molecular analysis to a winning recipe
  • Neural network building blocks: the neuron equation, autoencoders, LSTM and GRU, attention
  • A closing reflection on alignment and existential risk

Labs: discussion-led, no notebook

Module 2

Neural Network Implementation

Open lecture
  • A network as layers of neurons, and how deeper layers detect richer features
  • The activation rule: what every neuron outputs, written in general form
  • Forward propagation by hand, with a coffee roast and a handwritten digit
  • Stacking layers and thresholding the output into a decision
  • The same network in TensorFlow: define the layers, stack, train, predict
  • Writing dense() and sequential() yourself in NumPy

Labs: L0.1 · L1.1 · L1.2 · L2.1

Module 3

Activation Functions

Open lecture
  • The activation function g: linear, sigmoid and ReLU
  • GELU and its place in the feedforward block of transformers and GPT models
  • Choosing the output activation, and defaulting to ReLU for hidden layers
  • Why purely linear networks collapse into linear regression
  • Softmax for multiclass classification and its cross-entropy loss
  • The floating-point precision problem and numerically accurate losses
  • Multilabel classification with one sigmoid per output

Labs: L3.1

Module 4

Regularization, Bias and Variance

Open lecture
  • What it means to generalize, and why training error alone misleads
  • Training, cross-validation and test sets; measuring regression and classification error
  • Model selection: polynomial degree, network architecture, and the three-set workflow
  • Recognizing underfitting and overfitting from the error numbers
  • Regularization and how λ moves the bias-variance tradeoff
  • Baseline performance, learning curves, and what to try next
  • Bias and variance in large neural networks, and errors on skewed datasets

Labs: L4.1 · L4.2

Module 5

Advanced Neural Networks

Open lecture
  • The architecture landscape: CNNs, RNNs and LSTMs, Transformers, generative models, detection and segmentation
  • Attention Is All You Need: the encoder-decoder architecture and its results
  • Nested Learning, a recent research direction from Google Research
  • The Temporal Fusion Transformer for multi-horizon probabilistic forecasting

Labs: L5.1 · L5.2
